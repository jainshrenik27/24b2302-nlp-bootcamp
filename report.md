# Report (validation claims only; seed 42)

**Q1.** BM25 wins: claims 673 (nDCG@10 1.00 vs 0.39) and 155 (1.00 vs 0.63). The gold abstracts contain the claim's rare literal terms (LDL-cholesterol, BCL-2, c-Myc), which BM25 matches exactly; bge ranked a topically close but wrong abstract first. General wins: 463 (0.00 vs 1.00) and 766 (0.32 vs 1.00). These claims are paraphrases with little word overlap ("metastases ... targeted therapy" vs a mutational-landscape paper; "side effects" vs "pharmacotherapy"), so BM25 ranked the gold at 13 and 8. Overall the dense model wins 19 claims and BM25 wins 8.

**Q2.** The fine-tuned `gte-reranker-modernbert-base` (k=20) provided the largest gain, raising nDCG@10 from 0.937 (best hybrid) to 0.961. This significant improvement comes with a cost: its latency is about 418 ms per query. Before fine-tuning, the zero-shot reranker only achieved 0.9375, and larger k degraded performance. The best hybrid combination (BM25, bge-large, MedCPT) achieved 0.937 with minimal fusion latency (0.1 ms). Ultimately, the fine-tuned reranker at k=20 was the most effective method, albeit computationally heavier.

**Q3.** Yes, overfitting occurred. We LoRA fine-tuned `bge-large-en-v1.5`. With random negatives, train loss fell from 0.368 to 0.057 while validation nDCG@10 dropped from 0.933 (untuned) to 0.895 by epoch 3. With hard negatives, training nDCG@10 rose to 0.952, but validation nDCG@10 peaked at 0.920 (epoch 3), failing to beat the untuned model (0.933). The submitted files for Task 4 (`t4_random.trec` and `t4_hard.trec`) were produced using this `bge-large-en-v1.5` base model, keeping the best validation epoch (epoch 1 for random, epoch 3 for hard).

**Q4.** 0 out of 43 validation verification errors (0.0%) are retrieval misses, meaning the gold abstract is always in the top 10. Retrieval is clearly not the bottleneck. Most errors originate from the verifier itself: `entity_mismatch` (19), `needs_multiple_docs` (12), `other` (7), `numerical` (3), and `negation` (2). Validation F1 on (claim, doc, label) triples reached 0.757 (precision 0.80, recall 0.72) for the new abstract-level NLI model.

**Q5.** Since the abstract-level NLI verifier improved F1, the next step should address `entity_mismatch` (19 errors) and `needs_multiple_docs` (12 errors). To fix `entity_mismatch`, we could fine-tune with harder negative examples where claims have slightly different entities but similar contexts, teaching the model strict entity alignment. For `needs_multiple_docs`, which requires synthesizing information across different abstracts, we could introduce a multi-hop reasoning component or an LLM-based joint inference step over all retrieved documents, as independent NLI scoring struggles with disjoint evidence.
